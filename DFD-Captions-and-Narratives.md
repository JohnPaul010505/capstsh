## Figure 5

**Caption:** Figure 5: Data Flow Diagram of Existing System

**Narrative:**

> Figure 5 presents the data flow diagram of the existing system of Triple J Fitness Center.
> The existing system is composed of three processes, namely 1.0 Record Fitness Data,
> 2.0 Review Fitness Records, and 3.0 Evaluate Progress, and it exchanges data with two
> external entities: the Member and the Gym Trainer or Staff. All data in the existing
> system are stored manually in the Workout Log Sheet, the Body Measurement Record, the
> Calorie Estimate Sheet, the Trainer Feedback Notes, and the Progress Assessment.
>
> In the first process, the Member supplies the system with a workout entry containing the
> exercise name, the number of sets, and the number of repetitions, together with body
> measurements obtained manually using a weighing scale and tape measure, and a calorie
> intake estimate taken from online tools or personal estimation. The system records these
> into the Workout Log Sheet, the Body Measurement Record, and the Calorie Estimate Sheet.
>
> In the second process, the recorded log entries are retrieved and submitted to the trainer
> together with the review request of the member. The Gym Trainer or Staff provides written
> notes, and the system produces feedback notes and returns verbal advice and suggested
> adjustments to the member.
>
> In the third process, the reviewed findings are evaluated against the member's weekly
> logging consistency and satisfaction response. The system produces a progress assessment
> and returns the logging consistency result to the member. Because the entire process
> depends on manual recording and personal observation, the resulting assessment has no
> data-driven basis, which is the limitation that the proposed system addresses.

## Figure 6

**Caption:** Figure 6: Data Flow Diagram of Process 1 (Existing)

**Narrative:**

> Figure 6 represents the data flow diagram of process 1 of the existing system of Triple J
> Fitness Center. This diagram decomposes the Record Fitness Data process into three
> sub-processes: 1.1 Record Workout Entry, 1.2 Record Body Measurements, and 1.3 Estimate
> Calorie Intake.
>
> In sub-process 1.1, the Member provides a workout entry containing the exercise name,
> the number of sets, and the number of repetitions, which is written into the Workout Log
> Sheet. The resulting workout entry summary is passed to sub-process 1.2, where the
> Member records body measurements such as weight and height taken manually with a
> weighing scale and tape measure. These are written into the Body Measurement Record and
> the resulting measurement summary is passed to sub-process 1.3.
>
> In sub-process 1.3, the Member attempts to calculate calorie intake using online tools or
> personal estimation, and the resulting value is written into the Calorie Estimate Sheet.
> Because all three sub-processes are performed by hand, the records they produce are prone
> to missing days and inaccurate values, which frequently leaves no data available for
> review by the trainer.

## Figure 7

**Caption:** Figure 7: Data Flow Diagram of Process 2 (Existing)

**Narrative:**

> Figure 7 represents the data flow diagram of process 2 of the existing system of Triple J
> Fitness Center. This diagram decomposes the Review Fitness Records process into four
> sub-processes: 2.1 Submit Log Sheet to Trainer, 2.2 Check Trainer Availability, 2.3 Review
> Records Manually, and 2.4 Provide Verbal Feedback.
>
> In sub-process 2.1, the Member submits the log sheet together with a review request, while
> the recorded entries are retrieved from the Workout Log Sheet. The submitted log sheet is
> passed to sub-process 2.2, where the system determines whether the Gym Trainer or Staff has
> time to perform the review, based on the availability response of the trainer. If the
> trainer is unavailable, the member waits or comes back later.
>
> Once the trainer is available, the records for review are passed to sub-process 2.3, where
> the Gym Trainer or Staff reviews the records manually and the resulting review notes are
> written into the Trainer Feedback Notes. The reviewed findings are then passed to
> sub-process 2.4, where the trainer's written notes are converted into verbal advice and
> suggested adjustments, which are returned to the Member.
>
> Because the review depends entirely on the trainer's availability and personal judgement,
> the advice produced is not data-driven, and the member cannot verify whether the progress
> being made is accurate.

## Figure 8

**Caption:** Figure 8: Data Flow Diagram of Process 3 (Existing)

**Narrative:**

> Figure 8 represents the data flow diagram of process 3 of the existing system of Triple J
> Fitness Center. This diagram decomposes the Evaluate Progress process into four
> sub-processes: 3.1 Check Logging Consistency, 3.2 Apply Advice Without Monitoring, 3.3
> Assess Member Satisfaction, and 3.4 Decide Continuation or Abandonment.
>
> In sub-process 3.1, the Member's weekly log check is compared against the recorded entries
> retrieved from the Workout Log Sheet, producing a consistency result. This result is passed
> to sub-process 3.2, where the Member applies the advice previously received from the
> trainer without any formal monitoring, supported only by the observation notes of the Gym
> Trainer or Staff. The applied advice record is passed to sub-process 3.3.
>
> In sub-process 3.3, the Member provides a satisfaction response, which is evaluated against
> the applied advice record to produce a satisfaction result. This result is passed to
> sub-process 3.4, where the decision to continue or abandon tracking is made and the
> progress assessment is written into the Progress Assessment.
>
> Because the evaluation has no measurable baseline, the decision is based on the member's
> feeling rather than on recorded data. When the member is not satisfied, the outcome of the
> existing process is that the member abandons tracking altogether.

## Figure 9

**Caption:** Figure 9: Data Flow Diagram of Proposed System

**Narrative:**

> Figure 9 presents the data flow diagram of the proposed Intelligent Fitness Progress
> Monitoring Application Using Predictive Analytics. The proposed system is composed of five
> processes, namely 1.0 Manage Accounts and Attendance, 2.0 Manage Workout and Measurements,
> 3.0 Manage Nutrition and AI Food Analysis, 4.0 Generate Predictions and Goal Plans, and
> 5.0 Generate Reports and Communication. The system exchanges data with four external
> entities: the Member, the Trainer, the Admin, and the Google Gemini API.
>
> In process 1.0, the Admin provides enrollment details and trainer assignment requests,
> while the Member provides registration details and QR code check-in and check-out data.
> The process stores the profile records in the User Profiles, the enrollment and assignment
> records in the Enrollment and Assignment Records, the check-in record in the Check-In and
> Attendance Records, and returns the account confirmation, attendance record, assigned
> member list, and notification to the users.
>
> In process 2.0, the Member provides a workout entry and a body measurement entry. The
> process retrieves the MET value from the MET Exercise Catalog, computes the calories
> burned, and stores the workout record in the Workout Logs and the measurement record in
> the Body Measurements, while returning the calories burned and the BMI result to the
> member and the member workout summary to the trainer.
>
> In process 3.0, the Member provides a meal log entry and a food photograph. The food image
> and nutrition query are sent to the Google Gemini API, which returns the recognized food
> item and the nutrient breakdown data. The process stores the meal record in the Meal Logs
> and the analysis result in the AI Food Analysis, and returns the food recommendation and
> the nutrient breakdown to the member.
>
> In process 4.0, the workout, measurement, and nutrition histories are analysed to produce
> the prediction record and the goal plan record. The process returns the predicted fitness
> progress and the adjusted goal suggestion to the member, and the retention risk alert and
> the plan completion status to the trainer.
>
> In process 5.0, the chat messages and trainer ratings of the member, the feedback of the
> trainer, and the report request and broadcast content of the admin are processed to
> produce the progress report, the member report, the analytics report, and the inactive
> member alert.
>
> Overall, the data flow diagram of the proposed system shows that the manual recording and
> informal communication of the existing system are replaced by an automated platform that
> captures data through QR codes and mobile inputs, processes it through predefined and
> AI-based computations, stores it in a centralized cloud database, and returns accurate,
> data-driven outputs to the member, the trainer, and the admin.

**Caption:** Figure 11: Data Flow Diagram of Process 2 (Proposed)

**Narrative:**

> Figure 11 represents the data flow diagram of process 2 of the proposed system. This
> diagram decomposes the Manage Workout and Measurements process into six sub-processes:
> 2.1 Record Workout Log, 2.2 Estimate Calories Burned (MET), 2.3 Maintain Exercise Catalog,
> 2.4 Record Body Measurements, 2.5 Compute BMI, and 2.6 View Workout and BMI History.
>
> In sub-process 2.1, the Member provides a workout entry containing the exercise name, the
> number of sets, the number of repetitions, and the duration, which is written into the
> Workout Logs. The workout entry data are passed to sub-process 2.2, where the MET value is
> retrieved from the MET Exercise Catalog to compute the calories burned. The computed value
> is returned to the Member and stored in the Workout Logs as the total calories record.
>
> In sub-process 2.3, the Trainer provides the exercise verification and the exercise
> catalog record is maintained in the MET Exercise Catalog. In sub-process 2.4, the Member
> provides the body measurement entry, which is written into the Body Measurements together
> with the measured date.
>
> The measurement data are passed to sub-process 2.5, where the BMI is computed from the
> recorded height and weight, returned to the Member, and stored in the Body Measurements.
> In sub-process 2.6, the workout history data and the measurement history data are
> retrieved and returned to the Member as the workout and BMI history.

> recommendation, which is returned to the Member and stored in the AI Food Analysis.
